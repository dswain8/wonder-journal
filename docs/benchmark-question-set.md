# Wonder Journal Benchmark Question Set

This document defines the first 25 benchmark questions for Wonder Journal.

The benchmark set exists to do three things:

- protect factual quality
- keep explanations age-appropriate
- give the team a repeatable quality bar before expanding coverage

Each benchmark question should eventually have:

- a locked core fact
- an allowed simplification
- a banned oversimplification
- a reusable story angle
- a reusable scene plan
- a follow-up wonder question

## How To Use This Set

For every LLM or content change:

1. Generate answers for all benchmark questions.
2. Check factual correctness first.
3. Check whether the answer is understandable for ages 3-6.
4. Check whether the follow-up wonder keeps the topic open.
5. Check whether the scene tags map to reusable art.

If a change hurts benchmark quality, it should not ship.

## Evaluation Rubric

Each answer should be scored from 1-5 on:

- factual accuracy
- child comprehension
- delight / warmth
- brevity / pacing
- scene match quality
- follow-up wonder quality

## 1. Space

### BQ-01

- Question: `Why does the moon follow our car?`
- Core fact: The moon is so far away that it looks almost fixed while nearby things rush past.
- Allowed simplification: Far-away things seem to move slowly compared with nearby things.
- Avoid: `The moon is really following us.`
- Story angle: A night drive with trees, signs, and the moon outside the window.
- Scene tags: `moon`, `car`, `night`, `trees`, `distance`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why the moon looks bigger when it is near the horizon?`

### BQ-02

- Question: `Why do stars twinkle?`
- Core fact: Starlight bends a little as it passes through moving air in Earth's atmosphere, so stars seem to twinkle.
- Allowed simplification: Wiggly air makes starlight look sparkly.
- Avoid: `Stars blink on purpose.`
- Story angle: A rooftop or balcony star-watching moment.
- Scene tags: `stars`, `night`, `air`, `rooftop`, `sparkle`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why planets usually look steadier than stars?`

### BQ-03

- Question: `Why does the moon change shape?`
- Core fact: The moon does not really change shape; we see different lit parts as it moves around Earth.
- Allowed simplification: Sunlight lights different parts of the moon as it travels.
- Avoid: `The moon gets cut into pieces.`
- Story angle: A moon calendar with silver slices changing over time.
- Scene tags: `moon phases`, `night sky`, `sunlight`, `calendar`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why we can sometimes see the moon in the daytime?`

### BQ-04

- Question: `Why is the sky dark at night?`
- Core fact: At night our part of Earth turns away from the sun, so the sky is no longer lit by sunlight.
- Allowed simplification: Earth turns, and nighttime is when we face away from the sun.
- Avoid: `The sun goes away completely.`
- Story angle: Earth spinning like a slow toy globe.
- Scene tags: `earth`, `sun`, `night`, `globe`, `spinning`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why sunsets have so many colors?`

### BQ-05

- Question: `Why does the sun look bigger at sunset?`
- Core fact: The sun is not actually much bigger; our eyes and brain can make it seem larger near the horizon.
- Allowed simplification: It is an optical illusion near the ground.
- Avoid: `The sun swells up in the evening.`
- Story angle: Watching sunset from a field or hill.
- Scene tags: `sunset`, `horizon`, `field`, `orange sky`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why the sun looks orange near the ground?`

## 2. Weather And Nature

### BQ-06

- Question: `Why do peacocks dance in the rain?`
- Core fact: Peacocks often spread their feathers and display during wet weather, especially as part of courtship behavior.
- Allowed simplification: Rainy season is a time when peacocks often show off their feathers.
- Avoid: `Peacocks dance because raindrops tickle them.`
- Story angle: A peacock in a monsoon field opening its tail.
- Scene tags: `peacock`, `rain`, `field`, `feathers`, `monsoon`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why peacock feathers shine with so many colors?`

### BQ-07

- Question: `Where does the rain go after a storm?`
- Core fact: Rainwater soaks into the ground, flows into streams and oceans, or evaporates back into the air.
- Allowed simplification: Rain can sink, flow, or turn back into vapor.
- Avoid: `Rain disappears.`
- Story angle: Puddles, soil, drains, and sun after rain.
- Scene tags: `puddle`, `sun`, `soil`, `stream`, `clouds`
- Follow-up: `I wonder how clouds get full of water again?`

### BQ-08

- Question: `Why do rainbows have colors?`
- Core fact: Sunlight bends and spreads into different colors inside raindrops.
- Allowed simplification: Raindrops act a bit like tiny prisms.
- Avoid: `Rainbows are painted in the sky.`
- Story angle: A rainbow forming after sun returns.
- Scene tags: `rainbow`, `raindrops`, `sunlight`, `field`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why rainbows look like arches?`

### BQ-09

- Question: `Why does thunder come after lightning?`
- Core fact: Light reaches us faster than sound, so we see lightning before we hear thunder.
- Allowed simplification: Lightning and thunder happen together, but light gets to us first.
- Avoid: `Thunder starts later than lightning.`
- Story angle: A storm cloud with a window-view explanation.
- Scene tags: `storm`, `lightning`, `thunder`, `window`, `cloud`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why some lightning looks zig-zaggy?`

### BQ-10

- Question: `Why do leaves change color?`
- Core fact: In some seasons trees stop making as much green chlorophyll, so other colors become easier to see.
- Allowed simplification: When green fades, yellow, orange, and red can show more.
- Avoid: `Leaves decide to dress up.`
- Story angle: A tree changing through the seasons.
- Scene tags: `autumn`, `leaves`, `tree`, `season`, `colors`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why some trees stay green all year?`

## 3. Body

### BQ-11

- Question: `Why do we get hiccups?`
- Core fact: Hiccups happen when the diaphragm suddenly squeezes and the vocal cords close quickly.
- Allowed simplification: A breathing muscle makes a quick jump.
- Avoid: `Hiccups happen because someone forgot to swallow properly.`
- Story angle: A tiny jumpy drum under the ribs.
- Scene tags: `body`, `breathing`, `diaphragm`, `hiccup`, `tummy`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why hiccups usually stop by themselves?`

### BQ-12

- Question: `Why does my heart beat faster when I run?`
- Core fact: The heart pumps faster during exercise to move more oxygen-rich blood to the body.
- Allowed simplification: Running muscles need more oxygen, so the heart works faster.
- Avoid: `The heart gets scared when we run.`
- Story angle: A fast playground run with a thump-thump heart helper.
- Scene tags: `running`, `heart`, `playground`, `body`, `energy`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why breathing gets faster too?`

### BQ-13

- Question: `Why do we need sleep?`
- Core fact: Sleep helps the brain and body rest, grow, and organize what we learned.
- Allowed simplification: Sleep helps bodies recharge and brains sort the day.
- Avoid: `Sleep is only for resting your eyes.`
- Story angle: Nighttime body helpers tidying while the child sleeps.
- Scene tags: `sleep`, `bed`, `night`, `brain`, `dream`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why we dream?`

### BQ-14

- Question: `Why do tears come out when we cry?`
- Core fact: Tears help protect and moisten the eyes, and emotional crying is one kind of tear response.
- Allowed simplification: Eyes make tears for different reasons, including strong feelings.
- Avoid: `Tears are just sadness spilling out.`
- Story angle: Different tear helpers for dust, onions, and feelings.
- Scene tags: `eyes`, `tears`, `feelings`, `face`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why onions can make eyes watery too?`

### BQ-15

- Question: `Why do we have bones?`
- Core fact: Bones help give the body shape, protect important organs, and help us move.
- Allowed simplification: Bones are strong supports and protectors inside us.
- Avoid: `Bones are only for standing.`
- Story angle: A hidden building frame inside the body.
- Scene tags: `bones`, `skeleton`, `body`, `movement`, `strength`
- Follow-up: `I wonder how bones grow as kids get taller?`

## 5. Everyday Science

- Allowed simplification: Warmth gives ice enough energy to become water.
- Avoid: `Ice gets tired of being hard.`

- Scene tags: `ice`, `sun`, `water`, `melting`, `table`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why water can freeze again?`

- Core fact: Water trapped inside the kernel turns to steam, pressure builds, and the kernel bursts open.
- Allowed simplification: Tiny water inside gets hot and pushes the kernel open.
- Avoid: `Popcorn pops because it is excited.`

- Scene tags: `popcorn`, `kitchen`, `steam`, `heat`, `kernel`

- Allowed simplification: When light moves, shadows change too.

- Scene tags: `shadow`, `sun`, `playground`, `light`, `ground`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why shadows get longer in the evening?`

### BQ-24

- Question: `Why do bubbles shine with colors?`
- Core fact: Light reflecting from the thin soap film can interfere in ways that make rainbow-like colors appear.
- Allowed simplification: Thin bubble walls make light bounce in colorful ways.
- Avoid: `Bubbles are painted with rainbow dust.`

- Scene tags: `bubbles`, `sunlight`, `rainbow`, `yard`, `soap`

- Question: `Why does a magnet stick to the fridge?`
- Core fact: Magnets stick to some metals, like steel, because magnetic forces pull certain materials together.
- Allowed simplification: Some metals respond strongly to magnets.
- Avoid: `Magnets stick to everything metal.`

- Scene tags: `magnet`, `fridge`, `kitchen`, `steel`, `shapes`
- Follow-up: `I wonder why some spoons stick to magnets and some do not?`

## Content Review Notes

When reviewing benchmark answers, use these hard rules:

- Prefer simple truth over cute fantasy.
- Never answer a causal question with a purely decorative story.
- If the topic is uncertain, prefer a safe factual fallback rather than pretending certainty.
- For ages 3-6, the fact answer should usually fit in one or two spoken sentences.
- The story should support the fact, not compete with it.
